# Known issues

- **The game server runs the clock.** If it goes down, moves stop. Nobody can take the pool: after 12 blocks (about two hours) anyone can settle and the last king is paid. A page that cannot reach the server shows that wait and a Settle button when it is ready. A game nobody has opened holds no PUSD and nothing happens to it.
- **Moves from your own wallet can be late.** A move is judged when it reaches the server, and with a WizardConnect wallet your approval time counts. A last-second approval is refused as too late. The game wallet prepares coins ahead of time, so a click a couple of seconds before the end still works.
- **Outage Settle needs the game wallet.** When the server is down, the Settle button uses the game wallet to pay the network fee, so a player with only an own wallet cannot use it.
- **External wallet pairing (WizardConnect).** After pasting the link into a wallet, the page may keep showing the QR code until you reload it, and after a reload "Reconnecting to your wallet" may only finish once you switch to the wallet tab and back. We suspect the pairing library drops its relay connection while the tab is in the background and ignores older messages after reconnecting; this is unconfirmed. The game wallet does not use pairing.
- **Small PUSD coins are combined, up to a limit.** Refunds and change land as separate coins, so a wallet's PUSD splits up over a round. A move uses one coin when one covers the payment, otherwise several, largest first. The referee takes at most 10 wallet inputs per move (8 PUSD coins for a protect, which also needs the king's proof coin and maybe a fee coin; 9 for an open or a conquest). Only a wallet whose PUSD is spread over more tiny coins than that is asked to combine some first.
- **Game wallet limits.** Withdraw sends PUSD and BCH, but trophy NFTs cannot be sent out of the game wallet. Trophies can be reached by restoring the seed phrase in another wallet.
- **The fallback wait is an estimate.** The server's clock is exact, but the no-server fallback counts blocks, and blocks arrive at random (about 10 minutes on average), so its "about N minutes" can be off by a lot.
- **Public chipnet Electrum servers are few and slow.** Only one or two answer on the default secure WebSocket port, and their response time varies from a couple of seconds to ten. The app tries several servers and remembers the one that answered, but a total outage shows a "network servers are not responding" message. Browsers also print their own "WebSocket connection failed" lines for servers that do not answer; the page cannot hide those.
- **The server's clock must be right.** Deadlines come from the host's clock, so the host must keep its time synced.

- **Self-conquest with a second wallet (Kingdom v1).** The contract only blocks a king from conquering with the same key, as an accident guard. A king who uses a second wallet can conquer their own throne. It costs about 10% of the value net (returned through the pool if that wallet wins) and resets the protect counter and the clock. There is no design change for v1.
